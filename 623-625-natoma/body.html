The short version

Four flats at $2,400 a month each bring in $115,200 a year. After a 3% vacancy allowance and a full set of buyer-side expenses, including property tax reset to the purchase price, that leaves $69,891 of net income: a 5.01% cap rate at $1,395,000.

That is the floor, and two things lift it. First, the rents are below market. Zumper put San Francisco’s median one-bedroom asking rent at $4,400 in September. Each time a tenant leaves on their own, state law lets that flat re-lease at market. At a conservative $3,000, the same price yields 6.91%. Second, the 1,285 sf basement is space California requires the city to allow as an accessory dwelling unit.

The basement: a fifth unit is on the table

California law (Government Code §66323) requires San Francisco to allow accessory dwelling units inside the parts of an existing apartment building that are not used as living space: basements, storage rooms and garages. The city must allow at least one, and up to a quarter of the existing unit count. Here that means one. The Assessor records a 1,285 sf basement under this building.

Whether it works depends on ceiling height, light, exits and the permitting path. The path also decides whether the new unit falls under rent control. Treat it as an option this building gives you, not income you are paying for: it is in none of the numbers on this page.

How it reads

At $348,750 a unit the building is priced well below the city’s $444,000 average, which is right for a 1923 building of one-bedrooms. At 5.01% in place, it compares with the city’s 4.82% average cap rate. The rents are close to the Bay Area’s average in-place one-bedroom rent and far below what San Francisco is asking for vacant one-bedrooms today. That gap is the upside, and none of it is in the price.

03 · The pricing · 03

What rent control means here

The rents move slowly. Increases on sitting tenants are limited to the Rent Board’s annual figure, 1.6% this year. Underwrite today’s rents as the rents.

They reset when a tenancy ends on its own. Under the state’s Costa-Hawkins Act, a flat a tenant leaves voluntarily re-leases at market. That is the 6.91% column, and it arrives when it arrives. No responsible model times it.

Evictions need just cause. An owner who wants to live in one flat should take advice on owner move-in rules before writing. Not legal advice.

03 · The pricing · Due diligence

What to ask for before you write

  • The leases and the Rent Board file. Rent roll, move-in dates, deposits, any petitions or pass-throughs, and the building’s eviction and buyout history.
  • Operating history. Two years of actual expenses: insurance, water, sewer, garbage and repairs.
  • The 3R report and permit history. In particular, what is on record for the basement.
  • Seismic status. San Francisco’s mandatory soft-story programme covers wood-frame buildings of five or more units, so at four this building should fall outside it. Confirm against the city’s list.
  • Financing, early. A four-unit building is still residential lending, and an owner-occupant can buy with far less down than an investor.

Honest limits

  • The rents are as reported by the owner. They have not been verified against leases or a Rent Board history yet. Both come in due diligence.
  • The expenses are estimates until the owner replaces them. Insurance, water, sewer, garbage and repairs start as planning figures. Property tax is calculated at the FY2025–26 rate on the price, and direct assessments are estimated.
  • $3,000 is a planning rent, not a market study. The $4,400 citywide median covers all one-bedrooms, including new construction. An older SoMa flat will lease below it.
  • The price is set on income. It is not based on a grid of comparable building sales, and it is not an appraisal.
  • The basement unit is an option, not a plan. Ceiling height, exits, light and the permitting path all decide whether it can be built, and whether it would be rent-controlled.
  • Square footage differs by source. The Assessor records 2,570 sf above grade plus a 1,285 sf basement. Other published sources show 2,852 sf.
  • Nobody should write on this building without walking it. Condition, systems, foundation and roof are unassessed here. Not legal, tax or lending advice.
